## How to read this protocol

This file is generated by `es.protocol.main()`. It fixes, before either sealed window is opened, the hypotheses, the decision logic, the pins, the thresholds, the table shells and the code of two sealed tests. The sealed windows are read only by `es.sealed_pull.pull_sealed` and `es.sealed_run.run_sealed`, and both refuse to start unless `docs/sealed/REGISTRATION.md` names the SHA-256 of this file. In this file, "registered" and "registration" describe a design that is fixed here and recorded, with the SHA-256 of this file, in `docs/sealed/REGISTRATION.md`; they do not mean an entry in a third-party registry unless that record is an OSF registration (Rulings 140 and 141).

- **Part A** copies the registered design (spec v3, with amendments) word for word: the economic analysis (section 4), the statistics (section 5) and the sealed tests (sections 6.1 to 6.5), with the inline amendment notes the spec itself carries. Its headings and numbers are the spec's own. Three sentences of sections 6.1 and 6.5 that named OSF as the record of the design are rewritten (Ruling 140).
- **Parts B to D** state what the code does, rendered from the code and from `protocol_inputs.json`: the windows, the development inputs, the registered family and the way each analysis is run.
- **Part E** lists the controller rulings that changed or pinned an analysis after spec v3, one line each, with the ledger number as the evidence pointer.
- **Part F** binds the files and the code to this protocol by SHA-256. **Part G** gives the table shells. **Part H** is the knowledge statement. **Part I** lists the library versions.

**Order of authority.** Parts B to D and Part E describe the code that will run, and the code is bound to this protocol by hash. Where one of them and Part A differ, Parts B to E govern; each such difference is a ruling listed in Part E (for example Ruling 69 for the rotation set, Ruling 84(d) for the wording of an H-B rejection, Ruling 28 for the identity of the code).

## 4. Economic analysis

**Sign convention:** DART_t = P^DA_t − P^RT,settle_t in $/MWh. An INC earns +DART per MWh; a DEC earns −DART.

### 4.1 Settlement prices
- **ISO-NE and MISO:** the panel's final hourly real-time LMP, which is the settlement price.
- **CAISO:** the settlement leg is the hourly average of the four **15-minute FMM** LMPs. *(Amended 23 Oct, controller Rulings 34 and 41: the charge-code number first cited here was never verified and is not part of the registered design. The rule comes from the CAISO tariff, Section 11 as of 19 Nov 2025, §11.3.1–11.3.2, which liquidates virtual awards at the simple average of the four FMM LMPs; see docs/fee_rederivation_2026-10-23.md, source A5.)*
  - **Pull every FMM hour OASIS still serves, now, up to 2026-06-30**, including training rows. OASIS keeps only a rolling history (review N8).
  - **Missing FMM:** in a deployment window, CAISO P&L falls back to the RTD average and is flagged as approximate. In training rows, the direction rule falls back to the panel DART.
  - Results are also reported without CAISO.
- **NYISO:** the **integrated hourly real-time LBMP** (NYISO MIS `rtlbmp`), which is the price virtual transactions settle at, is the primary NYISO settlement series if it can be obtained for 2023-01 to 2026-06. Otherwise the panel's plain 5-minute mean is used and flagged.
- **Alerts and labels are unchanged.** They still use the panel series, so only P&L and the direction rule use the settlement leg.
- **Two things to disclose:**
  - RTD-based alerts understate CAISO skill when P&L is measured on FMM.
  - MISO RSG and CAISO BCR are charged on *net* virtual supply, so per-node fees overstate costs for a mixed INC/DEC position.

### 4.2 Positions and fees
- **Primary, the premium position.** For node n and hour of day h, d(n,h) = +1 (INC) if the mean DART on the training rows is ≥ 0, and −1 (DEC) otherwise. The position holds 1 MW every hour.
- **Checks:** always-INC and always-DEC.
- **Results are split by the position's spike exposure**, i.e. the sign of the training-window skew of d·DART for that node-hour. "Short spikes" means sitting out should help; "long spikes" means it should hurt.
- **Fees, per MWh while the position is open** (`docs/virtual_bidding_rules_and_costs.md`; sources dated no later than June 2026, I4):
  - **Primary:** the calibrated, side-specific scenario:

    | Market | INC | DEC |
    |---|---|---|
    | NYISO | 0.20 | 0.20 |
    | ISO-NE | 0.75 | 0.75 |
    | MISO | 0.25 | 0.15 |
    | CAISO | 0.55 | 0.15 |

    *(Superseded 23 Oct by controller Rulings 78 and 90. The calibrated fees are now NYISO 0.20/0.15, ISO-NE 0.70/0.70, MISO 0.15/0.05 and CAISO 0.55/0.15 (INC/DEC). Every level is the latest value published on or before 30 June 2026. MISO's Schedule 17 and 24 adders are omitted, because no compliant posting could be retrieved. The binding values are `es.positions.CALIBRATED_FEES`, and the audit record is `docs/fee_rederivation_2026-10-23.md`.)*

  - **Uniform sensitivity grid:** c ∈ {0, 0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 1.00}.
  - **Stress sensitivity:** fees double on days when the trailing 7-day mean |DART|, lagged 48 h, is in the node's training top decile.
- **Hourly P&L:** π_t = d·DART_t − c_t while the position is open. On a sat-out hour, P&L is 0.
- **Framing:** binary volatility management (Moreira and Muir 2017). As a descriptive check on development data, report whether premium per unit of risk (mean π / sd π) is lower in alerted hours than in other hours.

### 4.3 Maintenance protocols and cutoff policies

**Maintenance protocols** (these refit models):
- **Rolling-origin:** the model and scaler are refit at each analysis origin on all rows before the calibration window.
- **Frozen-origin:** trained on 2023, calibrated on 2024 in the same season, then applied unchanged.

**Cutoff policies** (these share one cached score set, I5):

| ID | Policy | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Fixed, held-out | The (1−α) quantile of calibration-window scores, placed once (split-conformal analogue). |
| P2 | Fixed, in-sample (positive control only) | The (1−α) quantile of in-sample scores on **training rows from the same season as deployment**. |
| P4 | Rolling-α | For each settlement day d, the (1−α) quantile of the node's scores over days d−W … d−1. W = 56 days is primary, because the v1 code already used 56 (`run_rolling_threshold.py`). W = 28 is a sensitivity. Pre-deployment scores fill the first window. This is the analogue of sliding-window non-exchangeable conformal prediction (Barber et al. 2023). |
| P5 | Self-correcting heuristic | τ_d = F_cal⁻¹(1 − α_d), with α_{d+1} = clip(α_d + η(α − r_d), 0.001, 0.5) and α_1 = α. η ∈ {0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.05} is chosen by minimum panel-median \|log₁₀R\| on the quarterly analogues (§6.2), with ties going to the smaller η. It is presented as inspired by adaptive conformal inference, with no coverage guarantee at T = 92. |
| P6 | Exact-α (benchmark) | The (1−α) quantile of the node's own deployment scores, computed after the fact. |
| P4@r* | Matched-activity diagnostic (benchmark) | P4 with its target rate root-found per node so that its realized alert count equals P1's. |

**Weighted conformal (Tibshirani et al. 2019) is left out.** It needs a model of the covariate shift between calibration and live windows. The rolling cutoff (P4) is the label-free counterpart that operators can run, and the domain-classifier monitor (§5.5) reports the size of the shift.

### 4.4 Measures

- **Aggregation for economic claims:** the pooled portfolio, 33 nodes at 1 MW each. Per-operator portfolios are secondary.
- **Settlement days** run in each market's settlement time (MISO in fixed EST).

**Primary measures, over all node-hours at matched activity**

| Measure | Definition |
|---|---|
| LA | Loss avoided: Σ over alerted hours of max(0, −π_t^open) |
| PG | Profit given up: Σ over alerted hours of max(0, π_t^open) |
| NET | LA − PG, which equals the change in total P&L |
| SELC | Stratified excess loss capture, meaning **timing within calendar cells**. With m_s alerted hours out of N_s and loss L_s in stratum s: **SELC = [Σ_s (captured loss_s − (m_s/N_s)·L_s)] / Σ_s L_s**. This is the loss captured beyond what random selection within each calendar stratum would capture at the same counts, and it has a closed form. **Primary strata:** node × decile of the calendar-only (G6) model's score within the window, i.e. a calendar propensity (review N4). **Sensitivity strata:** node × local hour × weekday class × month. *(Amended 23 Oct, controller Ruling 68: the primary and sensitivity strata are swapped. Primary is node × local hour × weekday class × month, and the decile strata are the sensitivity. Simulations by the Opus reviewer showed that decile strata bias SELC upward under a calendar-only null (+0.005 with no skill). The finer cells were unbiased, with no measured power cost. Gate G1 confirms or reverses this.)* |
| SNET | The stratified version of NET, defined the same way, reported in **$ per MW-day**, i.e. divided by (nodes × settlement days). |

**Secondary measures** (CIs labelled approximate)
- **ES5:** the mean of the k = max(1, ⌈0.05·D⌉) worst daily portfolio P&Ls, as a positive loss. **ESred** = ES5 without alerts minus ES5 with alerts.
- **CAP:** captured |DART| share. Excess capture is CAP minus the realized alert rate.
- **Descriptive:** ES10 and maximum drawdown. *(Amended 24 Oct, controller Ruling 119, G1 M14: development-only. The sealed tests do not compute or report them.)*
- EFF from v1 is dropped.

**Value function**
- Read (NET, ESred) for **dominance**.
- Scalar version: **J_λ = NET + λ·D·ESred**, with λ ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 1} registered.

**Decomposition** (estimand E1, reported with CIs and not tested):

V(P1) − V(P4@α) = [V(P1) − V(P4@r\*)] + [V(P4@r\*) − V(P4@α)]

- The first bracket is the **timing effect**: letting the cutoff float with risk, at the same alert count.
- The second bracket is the **level effect**: the cost or value of P1's realized activity level, compared with holding α.

### 4.5 Comparators at matched activity
- **Stratified random:** the closed-form expectation inside SELC and SNET.
- **Calendar-only model:** the G6 model's cutoff set after the fact to the same per-node alert count.
- **VOL** (trailing 168-hour mean |DART|, lagged 48 h): run through P1, P4 and P6, and compared at matched counts.

  |---|---|

  Compute the rejection rates of the H-E rotation test, single-quarter and pooled, at α/3 and 0.05.
- **(b) Parametric simulation.** Model each node's hourly score and P&L as a seasonal mean (hour of day × month), plus a daily AR(1) component, plus hourly noise. Fit this to the primary pipeline's out-of-sample scores and P&L for 2025-07 to 2026-06. *(Amended 23 Oct, controller Rulings 55g, 63, 64, 84a and 92a. The seasonal mean is hour × weekday class × month, falling back to hour × month for cells with fewer than 5 days. P&L noise is heteroskedastic by hour × weekday class in the heteroskedastic scenario. The daily AR(1) is corrected for cell-mean absorption by indirect inference, and the "upper" persistence scenario is the CI upper end of the corrected φ, with the stationary SD held fixed. The H-B size laws are fitted per block (DART3, LEV6, ROLL_DISP9), with no-drift and common-drift (0.25 and 0.5 score-SD) scenarios.   - Simulate two independent quarters under no skill, and under equal portability.
  - Measure the size of H-E and H-B at α/3 and 0.05.
  - Repeat at the upper end of the persistence CI.

This keeps a correctly sized test's false-demotion chance at 5% or below at the replication counts used. "Upper end of the persistence CI" means the per-node bound φ + 1.96·√((1 − φ²)/n), with the stationary SD held fixed. The H-B size check also includes an equal-portability-under-drift null, a common deployment shift of both blocks.)*

**Power**
- Use the same simulation, at **conservative** effect sizes: half the analogue point estimate.
- Use the AQ3 → A and AQ4 → B mapping, pooled for H-E.
- **H-E stays in the family whatever its power** (review N7). An H-B test with power below 0.5 becomes an estimand.

**No-drift context**
- The same simulation gives the no-drift distribution of failure counts per band, block and estimator.
- It is reported **descriptively** next to the observed counts. It is not a decision threshold (review N2).

Size, power, η and the no-drift context all go into the protocol.

### 6.3 Hypotheses (identical for Tests A and B)

**Primary family (Holm, one-sided, family-wise 0.05)**

| ID | Hypothesis | Test |
|---|---|---|
| H-E | **Timing within calendar cells:** alerts capture more loss than calendar-propensity-matched random selection at matched activity. | Pooled SELC over A ∪ B > 0, loss-weighted as (excess_A + excess_B) / (L_A + L_B). Pipeline: combined model (calendar plus all price history), XGBoost, rolling-origin, P1, premium position, calibrated fees. **Rotation test:** within each window, each node-hour's alerts are rotated by s whole days, s ∈ {14, …, D − 14}. A and B are rotated independently, and p is taken over all (s_A, s_B) pairs. *(Amended 23 Oct, controller Ruling 69: the shift set is the full cyclic group s ∈ {0, 1, …, D − 1}, i.e. min_shift = 1, because the {14 … D − 14} set is not a group and over-rejects under day-to-day persistence.)* *(Amended 23 Oct, controller Ruling 55, after the Opus review of the bootstrap code: each window's shift set includes its unshifted state 0, so p = (1 + #{non-identity grid cells with T ≥ T_obs}) / ∏(n_w + 1) is a valid randomization p; and the rotation statistic T is the pooled excess studentized by its within-stratum hypergeometric SD at each path's own counts. Pooled SELC remains the reported estimand.)* |

**Decision logic (registered, review N3)**
- **H-B_j** is *confirmed* if two conditions hold:
  - its Holm-adjusted test rejects in Test A;
  - Test B's statistic has the same sign, with one-sided p < 0.05 (replication).

  Other outcomes: "not replicated" if B disagrees; "confirmed in A, replication pending" if B has not run.
- **H-E** is decided only on the pooled window, so it needs Test B. Before Test B, A's single-window rotation p is reported as interim, not confirmatory.
- **Holm across tests run at different times.** The family's p-values arrive at two moments: H-B1 and H-B2 after Test A, H-E after Test B.
  - In Test A, an H-B test counts as rejected only if p ≤ 0.05/3. Holm would reject it under any later H-E p-value, so this decision is never overturned.
  - The full Holm step-down over {H-E, H-B1, H-B2} is applied once Test B is in. It can then also reject an H-B test with α/3 < p ≤ 0.05/2 or 0.05, depending on its rank.
  - The replication condition in Test B applies either way.
  - *(Amended 24 Oct, controller Ruling 118, after gate G1 (Seat M, M1; Seat C1, C1-I1).*
    - *The registration rule of §6.2 replaced H-B1 and H-B2 by estimands, so the registered primary family is {H-E} alone.*
    - *Holm over one hypothesis is a one-sided test at 0.05: H-E reads "timing skill within calendar cells detected" if and only if its pooled rotation p ≤ 0.05.*
    - *The three-member step-down, the 0.05/3 rule in Test A and the H-B rows of the interpretation table above apply only to a family in which an H-B hypothesis stayed confirmatory, which the registered family does not.)*
- **Headline numbers:** the pooled estimands E1–E5 are the headline whenever they exist. Before Test B, Test A's values are labelled interim. *(Amended 25 Oct, controller Ruling 109 M5 and G1 M23: E1–E4 pool Test A and Test B; E5, the block table, is computed per window and is not pooled.)*
- **Test B runs the registered code.** Any fix after Test A is a departure, and results are reported both ways.

### H-E as run (Rulings 55, 68 and 69)

- **Strata.** The primary SELC strata are node x local hour x weekday class x month (`market, node, settle_hour, wclass, month`), for the test statistic and for the headline pooled SELC. The sensitivity strata are node x decile of the calendar-only model's score within the window (`market, node, cal_decile`); E4 reports SELC under them (Ruling 68).
- **Statistic.** Within a window and a stratum s, let N_s be the rows, A_s the alerted rows, L_s their loss and Q_s the sum of squared losses. The excess is the sum over strata of the captured loss minus (A_s / N_s) L_s. The test statistic is T = (sum of the windows' excesses) / sqrt(sum of the windows' variances), where a window's variance is the sum over strata of A_s (N_s - A_s) / (N_s (N_s - 1)) x (Q_s - L_s^2 / N_s): the variance of the captured loss when A_s of the N_s rows are chosen at random within the stratum, at the path's own alert counts. T = 0 when that variance is 0. The reported estimand stays pooled SELC = (excess_A + excess_B) / (L_A + L_B) (Ruling 55).
- **Reference set.** In each window every node-hour's alert series is rotated circularly by the same whole number of days s in the full cyclic group {0, 1, ..., D - 1}, with s = 0 the observed state (D = 92 days for Test A, 92 for Test B). The two windows are rotated independently, and the p-value is p = (1 + the number of non-identity grid cells with T at least the observed T) / (the number of cells): 92 cells for Test A alone and 8464 for the pooled test, so the smallest attainable p is 1/92 for a single window and 1/8464 pooled (Rulings 55 and 69). If Test A's output is not available, Test B tests H-E on its own window alone (92 cells), and the result is not confirmatory (Rulings 112 and 116).
- **Validity.** The p-value is exact (a randomization p-value) under exchangeability of each window's observed alert path with its rotations, on a complete grid of days and node-hours. Where hours are missing, a rotation drops an alert whose target day and hour has no row, the rotations no longer form a group acting on the paths, and the guarantee is approximate; the share of alerts retained is reported for every window (`retained_share`, Ruling 55(e)).
- **What the null is not.** "Timing within calendar cells" includes any timing inside a month, among it the day-of-year seasonality that the model has learned from its training years; a rejection does not by itself show skill beyond the calendar. The paired E4 contrast of the alerts with the calendar-only model at the same count is the quantity that isolates timing beyond the calendar (Task 11 review M13).
- **What H-E measures (Ruling 118, Seat M, M12).** H-E tests the gross timing of losses within calendar cells, including timing that the model has learned from calendar features. It is not net value: the net quantities are the estimands NET and SNET (E1 and E2), and a rejection of H-E is not described as net value.

### Size study, power and placebo checks

- **H-E's size is at or near nominal.** The worst of the 4 rows that the rule reads for the pooled test (primary strata) rejects 0.0233 at 0.05/3 (7 of 300 replications; heteroskedastic, fit) and 0.0700 at 0.05. Pooled over those 4 rows, the rejection rate is 20/1200 = 0.0167 at 0.05/3 and 59/1200 = 0.0492 at 0.05. The protocol makes no claim that the test is conservative.
- **What the registration rule can detect (Ruling 118, Seat M, M13).** The rule demotes a row only when its rejection rate exceeds 1.5 times nominal and a one-sided binomial test of its count against nominal rejects at 0.05/4, the level divided by the 4 rows read. With 300 simulations per row that takes at least 12 of 300 (0.0400) at 0.05/3 and 25 of 300 (0.0833) at 0.05. A row whose true size is 1.5 times nominal is therefore demoted with probability 0.08 at 0.05/3 and 0.32 at 0.05 (the exact binomial tail). The rule is built against a false demotion rather than to catch a modest oversize. For H-E that costs nothing, because its pooled rows are at nominal.
- **Why H-B1 and H-B2 are estimands.** The size reasons in the table come from the calibration-and-deployment bootstrap under persistence, with and without a common deployment shift of both blocks (the common-drift rows of the size study, Rulings 63(b) and 84(b)). They are a property of the bootstrap, not a finding about the blocks.
- **Rows each H-B test reads** (Ruling 94, M3): H-B1 reads the laws DART3, LEV6; H-B2 reads the laws DART3, ROLL_DISP9.
- **Persistence correction.** The daily persistence of the simulated score and profit and loss is corrected by indirect inference (Ruling 84(a)): the simulation is run on the fit calendar, the same de-meaning and noise share are applied, and the lag-one autocorrelation is matched. The persistence "upper end" is phi + 1.96 sqrt((1 - phi^2) / n) per node, capped at 0.99, with the stationary SD held fixed (Ruling 63(a)). That upper end covers the true persistence in about 80 to 90 per cent of fits, because the corrected estimator's SD exceeds the formula's standard error (Task 17 re-review N-1); the size at the upper end is a sensitivity, not a bound.
- **Assumptions of the size study (Ruling 118, Seat M, M8).** The simulation draws the nodes independently, with Gaussian shocks and AR(1) days. A supplementary check by the G1 methodology review, which is not part of the registration rule and is not in the inputs, ran the registered rotation test (full cyclic group, studentized statistic, primary strata) on synthetic data of 12 nodes in 3 markets and two 92-day windows, 500 replications per scenario, under departures that the study does not contain: a market-wide common daily factor (0.8 of the daily variance) in the score and in the profit and loss, market-wide spike days, daily persistence 0.95, and independent trends within each window. The pooled rejection rates were at most 0.026 at 0.05/3 and 0.068 at 0.05, and the single-window rates at most 0.016 and 0.054; none is significantly above nominal on the registered binomial check (one-sided, at 0.05/4). The exact guarantee of the rotation test needs each window's alert path to be exchangeable with its rotations, so a drift within a window is the departure it is least protected against: the two trend scenarios gave the highest pooled rates at 0.05. The probes and their outputs are archived in `docs/reviews/g1_probes/`.
- **Placebo pairings.** Only the 1-year pairings count (Ruling 94): their profit and loss comes from the calibration year, outside the alert model's training window. The pooled 1-year check demotes H-E if and only if its p is at most 0.05/3. The 2-year pairings are descriptive: their P&L quarter lies inside the training window and the day-of-year features let the alerts reproduce that year's spike days, so a 2-year pairing is not a no-skill null. Alignment is by calendar date shifted one year, which equals day-of-quarter alignment except for one March day in leap-year pairings (Rulings 94 and M2).
- **Power.** H-E stays in the family whatever its power, and the rule never reads it; its power is reported as a curve over the coupling grid. The effect target (half of the pooled analogue SELC, which is negative in development) lies below the grid of couplings, so the power read at the lowest coupling does not describe a realistic effect. H-B power is read at the first Holm level at half the AQ3 median gap (Rulings 29, 84 and 94).

### E1 to E4: what each estimand measures (Rulings 81 and 118)

- **E4 (Ruling 81).** E4's registered quantities are the paired contrasts dSELC = SELC(P1) - SELC(comparator) for the comparators `calendar@count` and `VOL@count`: the calendar-only model's cutoff and VOL (the trailing 168-hour mean |DART|, lagged 48 hours), each set to P1's alert count per node. The contrast is computed inside every bootstrap replicate, so its interval is paired and uses the same resampled days. The comparators' own SELC levels are context.
- **E4's ESred rows (Ruling 118, Seat M, M6).** For each of the same comparators c, E4 also reports ESred(c) (`E4:ESred|c`, context) and the paired contrast dESred|c = ESred(P1) - ESred(c) (`E4:dESred|c`), computed inside every replicate like dSELC. dESred|c is the downside protection of the alerts beyond a comparator at matched activity: it separates what the alerts add from what a rule with the same number of alerts per node (the calendar-only model, or VOL) already removes. Both are tail measures, so their intervals are labelled approximate.
- **E4's sensitivity.** SELC of P1 under the decile strata (`E4:SELC_decile`) is a sensitivity only. It is biased upward under calendar-only nulls (the decile rows of the size study show the bias, Ruling 68), so no decision reads it (Ruling 118, M12).
- **E3 (ESred).** E3 is the tail effect of the policy, including plain exposure reduction and calendar allocation. It is not evidence of skill. The matched comparisons are SELC, SNET, E1's timing part and E4 (Ruling 118, M6).
- **E1's level effect.** E1 splits V(P1) - V(P4) into a timing part (P1 against P4@r*, which has P1's alert count) and a level part (P4@r* against P4). The level effect is a decomposition relative to P4, not a causal cost of a drifting cutoff (Ruling 118, M12).

### E5 and H-S: the interval of a level (Ruling 118)

- E5 (each block's median |log10 R| and failure count) and H-S (the failure counts of the recalibrated plain DART lags) are levels of one window, not contrasts. Their bootstrap is a calibration-and-deployment bootstrap, as H-B's is, with one difference: a single circular stationary draw over the quarter's calendar, indexed by (month, day), weights the calibration days and the deployment days alike. The two windows are the same calendar quarter a year apart, so every replicate keeps their matched calendar composition. The calendar index is the sorted union of the (month, day) positions of the two windows' days, so a day that only one window has (a leap day) takes its count from the same draw.
- Drawing the two windows independently, as the H-B bootstrap does, re-randomizes that composition. For any score with a calendar component, which every block has, the mismatch alone moves the cutoff against the deployment rate and inflates |log10 R| in the replicates, so the independent-draw interval measures the re-randomization of the calendar and not only the sampling variation of the quarter; for a score that is deterministic in the calendar it sits above a realised error that is near zero by construction. The comparison on development windows is in the probes archived in `docs/reviews/g1_probes/`.
- The primary interval of E5 and H-S is the aligned percentile interval: the `ci` field of H-S and, in an E5 row, `lo` and `hi` (the median |log10 R|) and `failures_lo` and `failures_hi` (the failures). The interval of the independent-draw scheme is stored beside it as a diagnostic: `ci_independent` for H-S, and `lo_independent`, `hi_independent`, `failures_lo_independent` and `failures_hi_independent` in an E5 row.
- Every E5 and H-S interval is labelled approximate (`approximate` is true whatever the NaN count): the size study evaluated the independent scheme, and the coverage of the aligned scheme is not studied. They are resampling ranges for the realised quarter, not intervals of studied coverage.
- H-B1, H-B2 and PC keep the independent scheme, the one the size study evaluated.

### Drift monitors (Rulings 66(b), 73, 83, 93, 104 and 109)

- Monitors: W1_norm, PSI, domain_auc, early_rate. The inputs of the monitors come from deployment days 1 to 14 (counted from each node's first deployment day), and the outcome, |log10 R| and node failure, from day 15 to the end of the window, with the calibration cutoff unchanged.
- The domain classifier is a balanced logistic regression with a class-stratified 5-fold cross-validation blocked by local settlement day (Ruling 73), which amends the "blocked by week" of spec section 5 item 5.
- The 1-Wasserstein monitor is the July pipeline's statistic (Ruling 44). With no drift the day-blocked AUC is somewhat above 0.5 for persistent inputs: about 0.55 for AR(0.9) features in simulation, on every window length tried, and up to about 0.9 for 168-hour rolling means (Ruling 93).
- The placebo reference columns (the same statistic on a split inside the calibration window) are descriptive: they are not monitors, have no p-value and enter no BH family (Ruling 83). Only ranks across nodes are interpreted.
- In the sealed run a node whose windows cannot carry the monitors (no calibration or deployment rows with complete features, fewer than 14 early days, or no later day), or that the block's score frame does not cover, is skipped and counted, not raised (Rulings 104(d) and 109, M3); the skipped nodes and their reasons are listed under `results["secondary"]["drift monitors"]["skipped_nodes"]`.
- The Spearman p-values of the drift-monitor family treat the nodes as independent, although nodes within an operator co-move, so they, and the BH decisions that use them, are optimistic (Ruling 40's caveat; spec section 5 item 2, Ruling 118, Seat M, M9).

### The sealed pull and the sealed run (Rulings 15, 28, 33, 58, 60, 70, 82, 104, 106, 109, 111, 112, 116, 118, 121 and 122)

**Pull** (`es.sealed_pull.pull_sealed`). It runs once per window and only after `verify_registration` has read `docs/sealed/REGISTRATION.md`, which it takes from `es.registration`'s own paths and never from a record handed to it. `verify_registration` also refuses a protocol that is not ready to register (Ruling 122, C2-1), so the pull refuses an unfilled knowledge statement before any download. NYISO, ISO-NE and MISO use the July downloaders unchanged with a new, explicit root; CAISO uses exact local-day UTC bounds, retries and an empty-result check, and pulls the FMM prices; NYISO's integrated real-time LBMP is pulled too. Each step is trimmed to the pull's own days and checked before it is recorded as done: on every local settlement day, every node of the node map must have exactly as many rows as the day has clock hours (24; 23 or 25 when the clocks change, MISO keeping fixed EST), every one of them on the hour (minute and second 0, in UTC) and with a finite price, in the price files and in the FMM and NYISO integrated tables (an FMM hour counts only with all four of its 15-minute intervals). A row off the hour is not an hour, and is logged. A short day, and a day with more rows than hours (an hour twice), both fail, and either is excused only by naming the day in `allow_missing_days`, which is logged. On a fall-back day NYISO, ISO-NE and the NYISO integrated table expect 23 hours, because the July NYISO and ISO-NE downloaders and the NYISO integrated parser drop both rows of the repeated hour; CAISO and its FMM table expect 25. CAISO's real-time hour is the mean of its 12 five-minute prices, and an hour with fewer than that, or with a price that is not finite, is NaN, so it is not an hour for this check (Ruling 116, Mn4). The pre-deployment rows are compared with the files already opened; the comparison is logged (`OVERLAP_CHECK.csv`, `PULL_LOG.txt`) and is not enforced, because ISOs revise settled prices.

The pull writes `PULL_LOG.txt`, `COVERAGE.csv` (`hours` per node, counting the rows on the hour that have finite prices, against `expected_hours`, with the count of non-finite rows), `OVERLAP_CHECK.csv`, the builders' logs under `logs/` and a `MANIFEST.sha256` over its folder. In `COVERAGE.csv`, `expected_hours` is the number of clock hours of the deployment window's local settlement days (23 on a spring-forward day, 25 on a fall-back day), which is not what the day check expects on a fall-back day. Test B holds one, 1 November 2026, so there a NYISO, ISO-NE or integrated node with the 23 hours of that day shows 2,207 of 2,209 hours, a share of 0.99909 and not 1; the coverage rule of the run (at least 90%) does not notice the difference. The pull is resumable but still once: raw responses are cached under `<root>/raw/`, finished steps in `.state.json`, and every attempt is logged. The NYISO integrated archive of a month has no interval grid to be short of, so it is cached whatever hours it holds and reused as it is on every resume; when the day check refuses the NYISO integrated table, its message names the cached archive of each month concerned, and deleting that file makes the next resume fetch the month again (Ruling 121, F4). A root that has files is resumed only from its `.state.json`; without it the pull is refused and never started over. A finished pull whose record in `PULLS.json` was not written (it was killed after its manifest) is adopted by a call with `resume=True`, which verifies its manifest and its log and downloads nothing. A completed pull is recorded in `docs/sealed/PULLS.json` (root, manifest SHA-256, times, protocol SHA-256 and the id and kind of the registration it ran under) and refuses any further call; Test B's pull needs Test A's completed pull, with a manifest that still verifies (Rulings 33, 58 and 106).

**A chunk that never comes complete (Rulings 111 and 121).** An answer that the server gave and that can be read is whole; it is refused if the validator refuses its rows (short of the interval grid, outside the bounds asked for, a price that is not finite) or if it holds no rows at all (OASIS's no-data report, a CSV without rows, a zip without a CSV). A network failure (a timeout, a connection error, an HTTP error status) and an answer that cannot be read are no whole answer. A chunk that gives no good answer in 8 attempts stops the pull, and `PULL_LOG.txt` names the chunk and says how many of its attempts were refused answers. A later attempt (`resume=True`) may name in `allow_incomplete_chunks` a chunk for which the log says that all 8 attempts were refused answers, and the chunk is then accepted only if every one of the attempts of that call brought a whole answer that was refused: the answer with the fewest problems (the last of equals) is accepted, short or with non-finite prices, and never cached, and a chunk whose answers held no rows is accepted as zero rows; if any attempt of the call was a network failure or an unreadable answer, the pull stops. Every missing or non-finite interval is logged, and the chunk and the reason go to `.state.json` and `PULLS.json`. A chunk that did not have all 8 attempts refused in an earlier attempt is requested and validated as usual, and the days that an accepted chunk leaves short still have to be named in `allow_missing_days`: two separate decisions, both logged.

- the test id must be `A` or `B`, and the run is refused if `docs/sealed/RUNS.json` already holds a record of the test or the claim file `RUN_<test>.claim` exists, whatever the output folder (Rulings 104(a) and 109, C1 and I9);
- the configuration is checked: the window end and the pull folders named are the test's own or earlier tests'. A configuration with a sealed arm must be the registered one (the rolling and frozen arms, the sealed pull folders and the window end of `default_config(test_id)`), must use the registered 2000 bootstrap replicates, must read `es.registration`'s own protocol and registration paths, must read the registered development data (its `dev_dirs` resolve to the registered folders, `DEV_DATA_DIRS`; other folders, or none, would end the run in its load or at the coverage rule), must write the registered output folder (`dart_dev/outputs/es/sealed/A` for Test A and `dart_dev/outputs/es/sealed/B` for Test B, the `out_dir` of `default_config(test_id)`) and, for Test B, read Test A's registered folder (`dart_dev/outputs/es/sealed/A`, the `out_dir` of `default_config("A")`), both compared as resolved paths, because Test B finds Test A's output through the folder that `RUNS.json["A"]` records and must be pointed at it (Ruling 122, N2b), and must take its code from the folder that `es` was imported from, so that the code manifest describes the code that runs (Ruling 109, C1, C2, I2 and M8; Ruling 116, N4). The run is refused too if the output folder exists: with no claim file for its test it was never locked, and the refusal says that nothing was read and to remove the folder and run again; if its log shows that the run took its claim or began its data load, the refusal says that the test counts (Ruling 116, N3);
- `verify_registration` reads the registration record and this protocol as they are at that moment: the record must be well formed (it names exactly one registration, which is an OSF registration, a public release of the code repository or the author's local record; a record that gives both a DOI and an OSF address must give the same registration in both), not dated in the future, and name this protocol's SHA-256, which the file must still have;

The refusal says that `PROTOCOL.md` is not ready to register, how many items are still open and the first two, and to fill in `docs/sealed/KNOWLEDGE_STATEMENT.md`, re-run `es.protocol.main()` and register that `PROTOCOL.md` (the SHA-256 in the `PROTOCOL.sha256` it writes) (Ruling 122, C2-1);
- `verify_bound` requires the pre-pull cutoffs, the protocol inputs, the code manifest and `ENV_PINS.json` to have the SHA-256 printed in Part F;
- the SHA-256 of every file of `CODE_MANIFEST.sha256` is recomputed: any difference refuses the run, unless `allow_departure=True`, which records the departing files (spec section 6.5 step 7; section 6.3, "Test B runs the registered code");
- the running environment is compared with `ENV_PINS.json`: a missing file refuses the run, and so does a different interpreter or a different version of python or of a pinned library; the interpreter is compared by the path it resolves to, so that python, python3 and a link to the same binary are one interpreter (Ruling 109(1), I7; Ruling 116, Mn2); the live environment is recorded in `results.json` and `RUNS.json`. `ENV_PINS.json` binds the interpreter path and the library versions of the environment in which this protocol was generated, the Anaconda installation at `/opt/anaconda3`: if it is updated, moved or reinstalled before Test B has run, in January 2027, so that the interpreter's path or the version of python or of a pinned library differs, Test B is refused before its lock until the environment is restored, so it must not be updated or moved until Test B has run (Ruling 122, C2-3);
- each sealed pull folder's manifest (the exact file set and hashes) is verified and bound to the verified registration through `PULLS.json` (Ruling 70): the record of the pull must name that folder, its manifest SHA-256, this protocol and the registration id of the verified registration (for an OSF registration the id stays the same when a DOI is added after an embargo; a public release and a local record have ids of their own, so a pull made under one record is not read under another), the pull's own `PULL_LOG.txt` must not name another registration id, and the pull must have started at or after `registered_utc`;
- the registered inputs are read from the bound `protocol_inputs.json`: eta, and for each of H-E, H-B1 and H-B2 its `confirmatory` flag, its `reasons` and its `approximate` flag (and H-E's `interim_approximate`);
- for Test B, the record of Test A is read. Test B is refused if Test A was never started, that is, if `docs/sealed/RUNS.json` has no record of Test A and the claim file `RUN_A.claim` does not exist (Ruling 112); Test B must be pointed at the folder that the record names, in every state of the record, and is refused otherwise; and it is refused if the output folder that Test A's record names is complete (it has its `MANIFEST.sha256`) but the record lacks `finished_utc` or `manifest_sha256`, with the repair to make by hand and a pointer to the recorded folder (Ruling 116, N2; Ruling 122, N2b). If Test A's output is available, its record, manifest, `results.json` and stored P1 rows are verified too (see Failure handling);

- at least 2 GiB must be free where the output folder lives and where `docs/sealed` lives, because a full disk after the data are read would spend the run (Ruling 109, I8);
- the folder that holds the once-only records (`docs/sealed`) and the parent of the output folder must be writable, because a refusal to write after the claim would spend the test without a row read; nothing has been created when this refuses (Ruling 116, N3).

**The lock.** The first thing the lock does is to claim the test id: `docs/sealed/RUN_<test>.claim` is created exclusively (`O_CREAT|O_EXCL`), so that of two runs started together only one gets it, and it is never removed. Then the output folder is created and the first lines of `RUN_LOG.txt` are written (the start time, the test, the protocol, the registration, the number of replicates, the hashes of the bound files and of the sealed pulls' manifests, the departures, the environment and, for Test B, how Test A stands), and the run is recorded in `docs/sealed/RUNS.json` (test, start time, protocol SHA-256, output folder, registration identifier, its kind and time, a fingerprint of the code, the number of replicates, the departures and the environment). All of this happens before any price row is read, and the claim is what the loader of the sealed panel requires: nothing reads the sealed panel without it. From the claim on this is the one run: a crash leaves the claim (and the folder, and the record once it is written) behind, and a second call is refused, whatever the output folder (Ruling 104(a), Ruling 109, I1 and I9; Ruling 116, N3). Because the claim comes first, an output folder that exists with no claim file for its test was never locked. When the run finishes, its record in `RUNS.json` gets `finished_utc`, its status and the SHA-256 of the output folder's manifest, which is what Test B checks Test A's output against (Ruling 109, I3).

**After the lock.**

- The data are the development files and the pull folders, restricted to the frozen node map, deduplicated by market, node and timestamp (first row kept) and cut at the window end in local settlement days. Rows whose timestamp is not on the hour are dropped and counted per market and node, because the panel build refuses them (Ruling 109, M2); so are rows whose day-ahead or real-time price is not finite (Ruling 82). The rows read and each kind of row dropped (outside the node map, duplicate, beyond the window end, off the hour, non-finite) are counted in `results.json` and `RUN_LOG.txt`, and none of them raises.
- Coverage rule (spec section 6.1): a node is usable with at least 90% of its expected deployment hours (`expected_hours` in `tables/coverage.csv`: the clock hours of the deployment window's local settlement days, as in the pull's `COVERAGE.csv`) and a feasible fit (at least 20 positive training labels). The test runs only if at least 28 nodes are usable and all four operators have one; otherwise the run writes `NOT_RUN.md` with the reasons and its status reads "not run (coverage rule)".
- The 34 score sets are fitted directly and never read from the score cache: the blocks G6, LEV6, MID3, DART3, MIDDART6, ROLL15, ROLL_DISP9, ROLL_LOC6, DART18, G1_24 under XGBoost (rolling-origin, seed 42); DART3, LEV6, ROLL_DISP9, G1_24 under logistic regression; G1_24, DART3, LEV6, ROLL_DISP9 on the frozen-origin arm; and those 4 pipelines again for the seeds 0, 1, 7, 13 (seed 42 is primary). The registered cutoffs are then compared with the cutoffs of these fits (`tau_check`).
- The bootstraps use 2000 replicates, the default of `run_sealed(n_rep=...)` and the registered value, which a sealed run must use (mean block 7 days, seed 20261022; `es.run_economics` N_REP = 2000).
- H-B1 and H-B2 are stored with their estimate (`t_hat`), their percentile interval (`ci`), their basic interval (`ci_basic`), the numbers of nodes and of NaN replicates and the field `interval_registered`. A hypothesis that is confirmatory in the registered family also has the one-sided basic bootstrap p-value (`p`), and its `interval_registered` reads "basic". An estimand has no `p`: its bootstrap p-value is kept as a diagnostic, under `diagnostics.bootstrap_p_one_sided`, its `interval_registered` reads "percentile (primary); basic (diagnostic)", and its status reads "estimand (registration rule)", followed by ": " and the reasons of the registration rule, in both tests (Ruling 109, I5 and M11; Ruling 110).
- E5 and H-S are stored with the calendar-aligned percentile interval as their primary interval and the interval of the independent-draw scheme beside it as a diagnostic (Ruling 118; see "E5 and H-S" above). H-S stores `ci` and `ci_independent` as lists. An E5 row stores flat columns: `lo` and `hi` (the median |log10 R|) and `failures_lo` and `failures_hi` (the failures) for the aligned interval, and `lo_independent`, `hi_independent`, `failures_lo_independent` and `failures_hi_independent` for the independent draws, with the NaN counts of each draw. Both carry `scheme` ("aligned") and `interval_registered` ("percentile, calendar-aligned (primary); independent draws (diagnostic)"), and `approximate` is true in every case, whatever the NaN count. H-B1, H-B2 and PC keep the independent scheme.
- The economic analysis (`economics_for`) is run with the development settlement tables and those of the sealed pulls. If it fails as a whole, H-E's rows are built on their own (`p1_rows_for`), in a guard of their own, so that one failing comparator or exposure split does not cost the run its one confirmatory test, and they are stored as `kept/P1.parquet`, which Test B pools H-E from (Ruling 109, I6; Ruling 116, N1).
- Comparator and policy rows are restricted to P1's rows and the dropped rows are counted (Ruling 82). Estimands are also computed without CAISO (Ruling 41).
- In Test A, E1 to E4 and H-E are computed on the quarter alone (`estimands_scope`: "single window (interim)"). In Test B they pool with Test A's stored rows when Test A's output is complete ("pooled A+B"); E1 to E4 are then also reported for the quarter alone, and the Holm step-down is applied over the confirmatory family of the registered inputs only, here {H-E} (Holm over one hypothesis is the test at 0.05; see Part D) (spec section 6.3). H-E is pooled from Test A's stored P1 rows alone: when Test A's other stored frames, the ones E1 to E4 are computed from, are missing, H-E is still pooled, E1 to E4 cover Test B's own quarter ("Test B window only (Test A's stored rows for E1-E4 are not available)") and `results["test_a"]["missing_kept"]` lists what is missing (Ruling 116, N1). When Test A's output is not available they are Test B's window alone ("Test B window alone (Test A's output is not available)"). E5, H-B, PC and H-S are always this run's own window, and `results.json` labels E5's scope (`E5_scope`: "Test A window" or "Test B window"; Ruling 109, M5).
- After its own lock, Test B reads each stored file of Test A through Test A's `MANIFEST.sha256`: the file must be listed there with the SHA-256 listed, or the analysis that reads it records the error (Ruling 116, Mn3).

### What Part A registers that the sealed run does not compute (Ruling 119)

Part A copies the design of spec v3 for the whole project, and the order of authority above applies: what this Part D says the sealed run computes is what it computes. Spec v3 carries an amendment note at each of the places below (Ruling 119, Seat M, M14).

- **Development-only; the sealed tests neither compute nor report them:** ES10 and maximum drawdown (spec section 4.4); precision and recall at the deployed cutoff against both labels (section 5 item 3; `es.run_labels_pr`); the mixed model and the leave-one-operator-out checks (section 5 item 1); the failure counts at the levels of alpha other than 5%, and the failure bands [0.8, 1.25] and [0.67, 1.5] (section 5 item 6). The sealed run counts failures only for the band [0.5, 2] at alpha = 5%.
- **Other levels of alpha that the sealed run does compute (Seat M, M23):** the audit-robustness family re-runs the H-B1 and H-B2 contrasts at the cutoff levels 1%, 2.5% and 10%, with the labels fixed at 5%, as descriptive diagnostics (the `es.secondary` docstring quoted under the secondary families above), and the economic analysis computes the economic curve over alpha of section 5 item 6, for P6 and P1.
- **From the reporting code, not registered computations:** figures, which are drawn after the run from the stored tables, and sign consistency across fees, positions and blocks, which is read from the stored estimates (section 5 item 2). The registered shells are the table shells of Part G.
- **Supplements, not bound by hash:** the development outputs, `outputs/es/analogs/` and `outputs/es/DIGEST.md`, are published with the code repository as supplementary files, instead of being uploaded to an OSF registration (section 6.5 step 3, Rulings 119 and 140). The code is identified by content, not by a git bundle (Ruling 28).

### Failure handling (Rulings 104, 109, 112, 116 and 122)

An exception in the data load, in the panel build or in the computation of the coverage table aborts the run (and a crash counts, as below); a coverage rule that is not met is no exception, but the registered NOT_RUN. Every analysis after the coverage rule runs in its own guard: an exception is logged in `RUN_LOG.txt`, recorded under the analysis' name in `results.json` (in `results["secondary"]` for a secondary analysis), and the others go on. The run ends with one of these statuses:

| Status (`results["status"]` in `results.json`) | When |
|---|---|
| "completed" | no analysis failed. |
| "completed with secondary errors" | only secondary analyses failed: the three Benjamini-Hochberg families (audit robustness, economic robustness, drift monitors) or their tables. The others are reported as usual. |
| "completed with errors" | an analysis outside the secondary families failed too (a score set, the cutoff check, a bootstrap, the economics, the estimands, H-E or the decision). The paper reports what completed and states what failed and why (`results["errors"]`, `RUN_LOG.txt`). |
| "not run (coverage rule)" | the coverage rule failed: `NOT_RUN.md` gives the reasons and no analysis ran, as registered (spec section 6.1). |

- **Coverage failure.** It means NOT_RUN, as registered; it is not an error. An analysis in which no node qualifies raises, and that is recorded as the analysis' own error without stopping the others (Ruling 104(f)).
- **Test B before Test A (Rulings 112 and 116, N2).** Test B is refused before its lock, and nothing is spent, unless Test A has been started: `docs/sealed/RUNS.json` holds a record of Test A, or Test A's claim file `RUN_A.claim` exists. A Test B that ran before Test A, by mistake, would lose the pooled confirmatory test of H-E, which a Test A run later could still have given. The folder that Test B is pointed at does not change this: a folder that looks like Test A's output, without a record of the run that made it, is not Test A's output. Test B is refused too when the output folder that Test A's record names is complete (it has its `MANIFEST.sha256`) but the record in `RUNS.json` lacks `finished_utc` or `manifest_sha256`: running alone would lose the pooled H-E for a state that no data read caused (Ruling 122, N2b). The refusal gives the repair, which is to set `finished_utc` and `status` in the record to the values in Test A's `results.json` and `manifest_sha256` to the SHA-256 of Test A's `MANIFEST.sha256`, and to run Test B again.
- **Test B without Test A's output (Rulings 109, 112 and 116).** Once Test A has been started, Test B runs even if Test A's output is not available: if Test A is NOT_RUN (its coverage rule failed), or was claimed and left no manifest and no finished record (it crashed, or its record was never completed), or finished without stored P1 rows, Test B runs alone. H-E is then tested on Test B's window alone and reported as not confirmatory, because the registered pooled test is unavailable: its status reads "not confirmatory (Test A's output is not available; the registered pooled test is unavailable)" and its wording "estimand (Test A's output is not available): SELC of the Test B window alone". E1 to E4 are Test B's window alone ("Test B window alone (Test A's output is not available)"), nothing is replicated and no Holm step-down is run, and a confirmatory H-B test reads "undetermined: Test A's output is not available, so there is no replication and no step-down". `results.json` says how Test A stood (`test_a`, with the state "pooled", "not run (coverage rule)", "finished, without stored P1 rows", "unfinished" or "claimed, not recorded").
- **Test A's other stored frames (Ruling 116, N1).** H-E is pooled from Test A's stored P1 rows alone. If P1 is there but the other stored frames that E1 to E4 are computed from are not, Test B still pools H-E, its E1 to E4 cover its own quarter ("Test B window only (Test A's stored rows for E1-E4 are not available)"), and `results["test_a"]["missing_kept"]` lists what is missing. Test A's fallback for a failed economic analysis stores its P1 rows (`kept/P1.parquet`), so that this pooling is possible.
- **Test B and the H-B estimands (Ruling 109).** Test B never depends on Test A's demoted (estimand) H-B results, and a failed Test A estimand does not block Test B: Test A's `p` and `t_hat` are needed only for an H-B hypothesis that is confirmatory in the registered family.
- **H-E fails in Test B.** The registered step-down needs H-E's p-value. If H-E did not complete, H-E is recorded as an error and no Holm decision is made: `results["holm"]` says so, and a confirmatory H-B test reads "undetermined: the Holm step-down needs H-E, which did not complete" (Ruling 104(c)).
- **Test A's stored output.** Where Test A's output is complete and its run happened, Test B pools H-E with Test A's stored P1 rows and, when the other stored frames are there, E1 to E4 with them (`kept/`), and checks them before its own lock: the record of Test A in `docs/sealed/RUNS.json` must name Test A's output folder, the SHA-256 of its manifest (which must still verify), its start time and this protocol; Test A's `results.json` must be Test A's own, of that start and under this protocol; and the stored P1 rows must be there (without them Test B runs alone). Stored rows that fail the check stop Test B before the lock, so that nothing is spent, and the paper then reports Test B as not run, with the reason. After its own lock Test B reads each stored file again through Test A's manifest (Ruling 116, Mn3). The last write of Test A's run puts `finished_utc`, its status and its manifest hash into its record; if that write fails, the output is complete, the error gives the fields to add to the record by hand, and until they are added Test B is refused (it does not run alone).
- **Interruption.** An interruption after the claim (Ctrl-C or a crash) spends the run, as registered: a run that started counts, its claim file `RUN_<test>.claim` (never removed) stays in `docs/sealed`, with its record once the record was written, and a second call is refused whatever the output folder (Ruling 104(a), Ruling 109, I9; Ruling 116, N3). A crash between the claim and the record leaves Test A as "claimed, not recorded" for Test B.
- **NaN replicates.** NaN bootstrap replicates are counted per statistic (`n_nan_replicates`), the p-value and the intervals are computed on the finite ones, and a result with more than 1% NaN replicates is marked approximate (Ruling 104(e)). Test B's decided H-E carries the approximate flag of the registered family (Ruling 104(b)).
- **Refused before any download and before the lock: the protocol is not ready to register (unfilled knowledge statement) (Ruling 122, C2-1).** `verify_registration` refuses a `PROTOCOL.md` that `es.protocol.ready_to_register` flags: an author marker (an `AUTHOR` placeholder of the template), an item-1 row of the knowledge statement without exactly one tick, or template guidance left in. Both sealed pulls and both sealed runs call it first, so none of them downloads or reads anything under such a protocol. The refusal says how many items are still open and names the first two, and says to fill in `docs/sealed/KNOWLEDGE_STATEMENT.md`, re-run `es.protocol.main()` and register that `PROTOCOL.md`; a draft that was registered with its statement unfilled cannot be used, because a registered protocol is immutable.
- **Disk space and write access (Rulings 109, I8, and 116, N3).** The run is refused before its lock, with nothing created, if less than 2 GiB is free where its output folder lives or where `docs/sealed` lives, or if `docs/sealed` or the parent of the output folder is not writable. The log lines of the analyses after the coverage rule are best effort: a write error there, such as a full disk, loses that line and does not end the run.
- **The environment (Ruling 122, C2-3).** `ENV_PINS.json` binds the interpreter path and the library versions of the environment in which this protocol was generated, the Anaconda installation at `/opt/anaconda3`. If that installation is updated, moved or reinstalled before Test B has run, in January 2027, so that the interpreter's path or the version of python or of a pinned library differs, Test B is refused before its lock, with nothing spent, until the environment is restored: do not update or move it until Test B has run.
- **Runbook.** Never open the sealed pull folders or the outputs in Finder: a `.DS_Store` file makes `verify_manifest` refuse.

## Part E. Refinements of the registered content

These controller rulings changed or pinned an analysis after spec v3. Each line gives the ruling's number, which is also the evidence pointer into the ledger named in brackets (dev: `dart_dev/.superpowers/sdd/2026-10-22-development-phase/progress.md`; 2A: `dart_dev/.superpowers/sdd/2026-10-22-plan-2a-sealed-machinery/progress.md`; 2B: `dart_dev/.superpowers/sdd/2026-10-23-plan-2b-manuscript/progress.md`; 2C: `dart_dev/.superpowers/sdd/2026-10-25-plan-2c-focus/progress.md`). The sections of Part D state the resulting behaviour in full.

- **Ruling 12** (dev): Precision lift divides by the node's realized evaluation-label prevalence, not by alpha (they differ by at most 1/n); spec section 5 item 3 is amended accordingly.
- **Ruling 15** (dev): The sealed entry point calls score_analysis directly and never reads the score cache.
- **Ruling 26** (dev): Calendar-propensity deciles are taken by value per node, so that tied scores share a stratum and the strata do not depend on row order; every public measure raises on an empty frame or a missing or NaN input instead of dropping rows silently.
- **Ruling 27** (dev): E1 to E4 carry day-bootstrap percentile intervals, and quarters pool by summing their additive pieces, each quarter resampled independently over days with the alerts fixed on the real path; E1 also covers J_lambda for every registered lambda; E2 is SNET.
- **Ruling 28** (dev): The code is identified by content (the SHA-256 of a deterministic archive and a per-file SHA-256 manifest), not by a git commit; if the author commits before registering, the commit and a bundle are recorded as well (spec section 6.5 step 3).
- **Ruling 29** (dev): The registration family is computed from the size and power study by a registered rule, never decided by hand: the pooled size governs the confirmatory H-E, the single-window size only labels Test A's interim p-value, H-B power is read at 0.05/3, and an interval is labelled approximate when the simulated size exceeds nominal (refined by Rulings 63, 92 and 94).
- **Ruling 30** (dev): Every cutoff of the confirmatory pipelines uses training and calibration rows only, so the cutoffs are computed before the pull and registered in `pre_pull_cutoffs.csv`; the sealed run recomputes them and reports any difference.
